Jargon Buster

          

            
SSD
Solid state drive. Where your files and programs live, with no moving parts. Much faster than an old hard drive, and the single best upgrade for a slow computer.

            
Hard drive (HDD)
The older kind of storage, with a spinning disk inside. Cheap and roomy, but slow, and it wears out.

            
Memory (RAM)
The computer's short-term working space. More of it means you can have more things open at once without everything slowing down.

            
Processor (CPU)
The brain. It does the actual thinking. Faster ones matter most for video editing and games.

            
Graphics card (GPU)
A part dedicated to drawing what's on screen. Essential for gaming, helpful for video work, unnecessary for email.

            
Motherboard
The main circuit board that everything else plugs into.

            
Power supply (PSU)
The box that turns mains electricity into what the computer needs. A common reason a desktop won't turn on.

            
Operating system
The software that runs the computer itself, such as Windows, macOS or Android.

            
Driver
A small piece of software that lets the computer talk to a device such as a printer.

            
Router
The box from your internet provider that shares the connection around your home, usually by Wi-Fi.

            
Ethernet
A network cable. Faster and more reliable than Wi-Fi when you can run one.

            
Homeplug
A pair of adapters that carry your internet through the mains wiring to a room the Wi-Fi doesn't reach.

            
NAS
Network attached storage. A shared drive on your home or office network that everyone can save to and back up to.

            
Malware
Any software designed to cause harm. Viruses, spyware and adware are all types of malware.

            
Spyware
Software that secretly watches what you do, often to steal passwords or bank details.

            
Adware
Software that fills your screen with adverts and pop-ups and slows everything down.

            
Backup
A second copy of your files kept somewhere else, so losing the computer doesn't mean losing the photos.

            
The cloud
Someone else's computers, reached over the internet. Handy for backups and for getting at files from anywhere.

            
Refurbished
A used computer that has been checked, cleaned and restored to full working order. Often excellent value.

            
HDMI
The cable that carries picture and sound to a TV or monitor.

            
USB-C
The small oval connector that plugs in either way up. Used for charging, data and sometimes screens.

            
Blue screen
Windows' way of saying something has gone badly wrong and it has had to stop. Worth getting checked if it keeps happening.
